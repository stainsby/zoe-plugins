**Adopter notes.**

1. **Director-specific index and state.** A new term in the instructions (*Terms*): what is
   true only for one director's environment and intentions — a path, a remote, a credential, a
   tool installed here, a note of where they had got to — is *director-specific*, stays out of
   the shared index and state, and lives in that director's own index and state, kept apart
   from what is shared. It matters only where more than one director runs the enterprise and
   its assets may be distributed among them. `zoe-setup` asks, where there is more than one
   director, where each keeps theirs, and encourages each to keep a single copy of the
   enterprise's files; `zoe-orient` step 3 reads the director-specific index after the shared
   one. No index field is added.
   **What to do:** one director on one machine — nothing. A team — agree where each director's
   own index and state are kept (outside anything shared; git-ignored, in a repository), record
   it in the index, and move any line in the shared index or state that is true only on one
   machine into the director-specific one.
2. **Every session records its open and close in the log,** naming the director and the kind
   of session (*Terms*, the `management session` entry; `zoe-orient` steps 6 and 7). A session
   with no close on record is open, and another session does no management-grade writing until
   it is closed or a director says it was abandoned. Orient states the kind of session and its
   trigger to a director who is present, before handing off, so they can choose otherwise; it
   asks only when it cannot tell.
   **What to do:** nothing in your records; your log simply gains an open and a close entry per
   session from now on. If an own skill of yours already says how a session opens or closes,
   re-read it against `zoe-orient` steps 6 and 7.
3. **A joining director.** `zoe-orient` step 3: where the index says directors keep
   director-specific records and none are present, orient asks who is present. A director
   already in the index is working in a new copy of the files, and gets their records created.
   Anyone else has just joined: they are added to the index's directors, set up, and the log
   records that they joined, so every other director sees it. Access to the enterprise's files
   is enough; the kernel imposes no approval, and a charter's own rules may.
   **What to do:** nothing. On a team, expect the question the first time a session runs in a
   fresh copy.
4. **The diet.** Every skill's header is now the one line `> ZOE Core file — read-only.`; the
   instructions' *Setting yourself up*, *Verification* and *Communicating with directors*
   sections, and `zoe-setup`, `zoe-orient`, `zoe-redesign` and `zoe-upgrade`, are the most
   shortened. Rules that were stated in several places — the kernel being read-only, a gate
   halting only the gated item, a check being separate from what it checks — lose their
   duplicate statements, and `zoe-setup` re-asks fewer of the questions the index template's
   placeholders already ask. Compare the instructions and `zoe-setup` first; then `zoe-orient`.
   **What to do:** if an own skill of yours quotes or cites a kernel sentence, re-read it
   against the new wording — sentences moved and some were merged, none reversed. The index
   template's placeholder text is shorter in four fields; no field was added, removed or
   renamed, so no index migration is owed.
